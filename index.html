ABOUT THIS EXPERIMENT

Follow the water. Follow the sediment.

Waves move water through a shallow coastal basin. The submerged root geometry contributes resistance where it is submerged. The resulting flow carries sediment, which can settle or erode the bed.

  1. Erosion: water applies bed shear; sediment is lifted when the erosion threshold is exceeded.
  2. Retention: suspended sediment settles in calmer water. Settled mass is added to the bed.
  3. Stability: a fixed reference water level separates actual shoreline change from a moving tide.
  4. Shelter: submerged areas with time-averaged flow below 0.12 m/s indicate potential hydraulic refuge, not animal survival.

Both coasts start identically. Only root resistance differs. Restart to apply new wave and water-level conditions fairly.

Gold tracers are enlarged observation markers carried by the computed water velocity. They are not individual sediment grains and do not add or remove sediment. Side-by-side cameras and clocks are synchronized.

What this model leaves out

The solver averages flow over water depth. Small root wakes, vertical turbulence, underground root reinforcement, growth and animal behaviour are not resolved. Sediment coefficients are illustrative, not calibrated to a real coast. Speed runs the entire calculation faster; it does not exaggerate erosion.
